## A Search Epiphany

Across last week I have been working on a project to relearn some Classical guitar songs I knew. I reached one song I had to learn and thankfully I had a video recording of me playing, like 10 years ago. I had no sheet music and I named the video with the wrong song. This led me down the path of the search, the failure, and the epiphany of a way to search when stuck. I guess I should start with a quick run down of where I was this week.

### The Need For a Search

I’ve been working on this project to relearn most of the songs I used to know in Classical guitar (Maybe a subject for its own blog). This week I reached the milestone of 7 relearned songs and today 11/09/25, I have begun the process of learning the 8th. All I had was the video of me playing on my youtube channel. A mess, I won't even share here, maybe someday I’ll clean it up. First thing I usually do is look up for a free pdf of the song score by name. Easy, right, not quite. When I searched I realized that I probably didn’t know the name of the song then and put the wrong one in the title.

### The First Failure

Realizing that the title was wrong I began a simple search. Like what songs are similar to this Bach song for guitar. At least I had somewhere to start. I knew the author. But think about it. How many songs Bach wrote? I’ll let you do a search on that. Didn’t really go too far on it. My better guess on the approach was, let's start on plan B. 

### Plan B, Even If You Don’t Have One Make One Up

Plan B is simple. I have the video and a good pair of ears. I grew up in an age where I had to pick up stuff by ear  and didn’t have a whole lot of tools or tutorials to help. I usually do a half and half approach even if I find the score. The reason for that is that sometimes I play things in my own way… Alright this is off topic lets go back. Learning by ear is hard and especially for Bach. He does everything on counter points with melodies that move in different directions with their own behaviors. If picture music was visual. To see everything that was done you would need chameleon eyes that move independently. Never mind that for some instance Spider/Bee eyes with chameleon’s degree of freedom. Jokes apart, it is hard.

### Plan A is Faster Try it Again

So while at the gym I was frustrated with my last attempt. I actually learned the first 5 seconds on Plan B, and while doing that discovered that I was playing in a different guitar tuning which made this just so much harder. I started to think, (someday I should write about how I use the gym to exercise thinking and let my brain go wild) How can I solve this problem. Plan A is just that much better. The reference is there, I can forget and always get back to it. I can read in smaller chunks. I don’t know being able to read and write music is something I should be exercising as much as I can. 

### Epiphany

So I used Shazam to do it! Good idea, right? Not so much I say. It found a Techno song and some other weird stuff unrelated and finally said, Sorry can’t find it. All I needed was a name but all I had was a video I made with the wrong title. I went up to different machines and listened to Tech podcasts and reading coding framework docs and something came to my mind. Wait a minute, my search is too broad. I have to nest this, remove its degrees of freedom. So my search was to give me a list of Bach songs that are arranged for Classical Guitar, Sort the list by popularity. It gave me a List of 10 songs. I then asked. Give me links to video rendition of these songs. While listening to these songs I found what I needed. 

### Lesson of The Day

Sometimes we need something that is specific. We know exactly what we need but we can’t really find it. Tools need some fine tuning to give you a smaller sample of what to look for. Even with a smaller sample some things can’t be done by smart machines. You have to walk some of the way on your own. You can use stochastic methods to get deterministic results like the name of a song. In the end only I could say which one was the right one but a brute force approach is not best. Sometimes a collage of methods is what is needed. Creating a list of songs was brute force; Ordering by popularity stochastic; Listening to the 10 first to find the right one Deterministic. So the best approach may be a mix of different approaches.
